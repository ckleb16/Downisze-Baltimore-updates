# Aging in Place Page Direction for Manus

## Overall feel

This page should feel warm, thoughtful, educational, and reassuring.

It should **not** feel like a real estate sales page.

The message is that aging in place is about planning early, understanding the whole picture, and making thoughtful decisions about whether the current home can continue to support someone well.

The page should establish Mary as a trusted aging and housing resource first, and a real estate professional second.

---

## HERO SECTION

### Headline

**Age in Place by Design, Not by Default.**

### Supporting copy

Wanting to stay in your home and being set up to stay in your home are two very different things.

Aging in place successfully means looking beyond where you live today and thinking about how your home, health, mobility, finances, support system, and community may need to work for you in the years ahead.

## SECTION 2: THE GAP BETWEEN WANTING TO STAY AND BEING READY TO STAY

Use two simple statistics visually rather than a long paragraph.

**75%**
of adults age 50+ say they want to remain in their current homes as they age.

**Less than 4%**
of U.S. homes have three basic accessibility features: a no-step entrance, single-floor living, and doors and hallways wide enough to accommodate a wheelchair.

Source the statistics discreetly to AARP and the Harvard Joint Center for Housing Studies.

Do not use a Baltimore-specific percentage unless we later locate a reliable source.

Baltimore can be referenced conversationally as a community with many older, multi-level homes and rowhomes that may present additional challenges for aging in place.

## SECTION 3: WHY THIS WORK MATTERS TO MARY

### Suggested headline

**For Me, This Is Personal.**

Mary's interest in aging in place began while helping care for her grandfather.

He had lived in his home for decades, but the house itself eventually became part of the challenge. Laundry was in the basement. Bedrooms and bathrooms were upstairs. Steps were required at the entrances.

That experience made Mary realize how often families begin thinking about housing only after circumstances have changed.

Today Mary is the **Founder and Chair of the National Aging in Place Council, Greater Baltimore Chapter**, bringing together professionals across housing, healthcare, finance, home care, wellness, and other aging-related services.

She also leads educational programs throughout Greater Baltimore, including **Should I Stay or Should I Go?**, designed to help older adults and families understand their options before a crisis makes the decision for them.

Keep the grandfather story concise here. The fuller personal story and authorship can live on Meet Mary.

---

## SECTION 4: START WITH THE HOME

### Headline

**Can Your Home Continue to Work for You?**

Use clean icons or short visual blocks rather than long copy.

Topics:

**Entry & Mobility**
Steps, thresholds, handrails, no-step entry, wider doorways

**Bathroom Safety**
Curbless showers, properly installed grab bars, non-slip surfaces, lighting

**Main-Level Living**
Bedroom, full bath, laundry, and daily living needs accessible without stairs

**Kitchen & Daily Function**
Storage, lighting, appliances, controls, and ease of use

**Maintenance**
Yard work, exterior upkeep, repairs, and the ongoing demands of the property

**Technology**
Smart lighting, medical alerts, voice technology, remote monitoring, and other AgeTech solutions

Keep this overview short. More detailed home safety information should link to the Resource Center.

* Transportation

## SECTION 6: TWO WAYS TO AGE IN PLACE

### Headline

**Sometimes Staying Means Changing the House. Sometimes It Means Changing Houses.**

Create two side-by-side pathways.

### STAY IN YOUR CURRENT HOME

Possible solutions may include:

* Home safety improvements
* Bathroom modifications
* Main-level living changes
* Stairlifts or elevators
* Home care
* Smart home technology
* Occupational therapy home assessments
* ADUs or multigenerational additions

### MOVE TO A HOME THAT BETTER SUPPORTS YOU

Possible options may include:

* One-level homes
* Ranchers
* Villas
* Condominiums
* 55+ communities
* Accessible homes
* Multigenerational housing
* A home closer to family, healthcare, transportation, and community

Important message:

**Aging in place does not always mean remaining in the same house forever.**

The goal is to find the environment that best supports how someone wants to live.

# DESIGN NOTE

Please avoid turning this into a giant information page.

The page should feel spacious and easy to scan.

Use:

* short sections
* strong headings
* large statistics
* icons
* buttons into the Resource Center
* ample white space
* Mary's real photography

The goal is for someone to finish the page thinking:

**“She understands the whole picture, and I can talk to her without being pressured to move.”**
