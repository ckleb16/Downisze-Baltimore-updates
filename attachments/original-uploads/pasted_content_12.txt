6. MEET MARY: POP STORY
This is the emotional center of the Meet Mary page.
Use the childhood photograph of little Mary with Pop on the boat.
This should be the only photograph on the website given a subtle vintage Polaroid/family-album treatment.
Use a simple white border with a slightly deeper bottom margin, a very subtle shadow and, if appropriate, an extremely slight rotation.
Do not heavily restore, modernize or AI-enhance the photograph. Its age is part of the story.
Suggested caption:
Me & Pop. Long before I knew where this would lead.
The story should communicate that Pop was technically Mary's grandfather but functioned as her father figure, her constant and her anchor.
He spent nearly ten years caring for Mary's grandmother while she lived with dementia. Later, after Mary's grandmother died and Mary had several wonderful years with Pop again, he was diagnosed with cancer.
Mary eventually became his caregiver while raising teenagers. A hospital bed was placed in the living/dining area of her home because that was where it could fit.
The experience left Mary asking:
How did I not know this?
She had spent decades helping other people with housing, yet neither Pop's home nor her own was prepared for what their family suddenly needed.
Keep this loving, reflective and hopeful. Do not make it fear-based.

Preserve and emphasize this philosophy:
Age isn't the deciding factor. The whole picture is.

Sometimes it is modifying the home.

Mary's role is to help people understand their choices before circumstances make the choice for them.
