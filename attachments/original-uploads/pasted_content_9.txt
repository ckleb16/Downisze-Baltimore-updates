Suggested small handwritten-style caption:
Me & Pop. Long before I knew where this would lead.
Tell the condensed version of Pop's story and his influence on Mary. He was her grandfather but very much her father figure, her anchor and one of the most important people in her life. He spent years caring for Mary's grandmother as she lived with dementia and was deeply committed to keeping her at home.
After Mary's grandmother died, Mary and Pop had several wonderful years together. Later, Pop was diagnosed with cancer. Mary became his caregiver while also raising high-school-aged children. He ultimately moved into Mary's home, where a hospital bed was placed in the living/dining area because the house had never been designed for caregiving or aging.
That experience changed the direction of Mary's work. She remembers thinking: How did I not know this? I've spent my career advising people about homes, and neither his home nor mine was prepared for this.
Keep this section loving and hopeful. It is not meant to frighten people about aging. The point is that better information and earlier planning can give families more choices.

5. FROM EXPERIENCE TO PURPOSE
Transition from Pop's story into the work Mary does today.
Mary's goal is not to convince people to move. It is to help them understand their choices.
Staying home may absolutely be the right answer. But Mary looks beyond someone's age and asks the bigger questions: Is the home accessible? What does the support system look like? Is there social connection? Transportation? Maintenance? Financial sustainability? What happens if circumstances change?
The goal is to help people make decisions while they still have choices and autonomy rather than waiting for a crisis to make the decision for them.
