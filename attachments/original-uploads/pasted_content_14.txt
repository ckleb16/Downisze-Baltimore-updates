## 2. “Should I Stay or Should I Go?” Interactive Self-Evaluation

Please turn the existing **Should I Stay or Should I Go?** resource into a short interactive self-evaluation on the website.

This is **not** intended to tell someone whether they should move. It is designed to help visitors identify what is working well, what may require planning, and what deserves a closer look.

### Intro

**Should I Stay or Should I Go?**

**A quick check-in on how well your home and your life still fit.**

Your home is only one part of the picture.

These questions are designed to help you look at your home, your health, your finances, your connections and your everyday life. There are no right or wrong answers, and this isn't a test that tells you whether you should move.

It's simply a way to see what's working well, what may need some planning, and what deserves a closer look.

### Response choices for Questions 1–9

* **Yes, this works well for me**
* **Mostly, but I should plan ahead**
* **No, this is becoming difficult**

### Questions

**1. Can your home physically support you now and later?**
I can comfortably enter and move throughout my home, use the bathroom safely, manage the stairs, and access the spaces I need. If my mobility changed, my home could reasonably be modified to support me.

**2. Is taking care of your home still manageable?**
Repairs, cleaning, yardwork, seasonal maintenance and everyday responsibilities feel manageable. I'm not ignoring significant repairs or depending heavily on others just to keep up with the house.

**3. Can you get where you need and want to go?**
I can comfortably get to groceries, medical appointments, friends, activities and the places that matter to me. I also have realistic transportation options if driving becomes difficult, especially at night.

**4. Does where you live support your health?**
I can easily access healthcare, nutritious food and the services I need. My daily environment makes it reasonably easy for me to eat well and take care of myself.

**5. Does your everyday life help you stay active?**
I have realistic opportunities to walk, exercise, maintain strength and keep moving in ways I enjoy.

**6. Are you as connected as you'd like to be?**
I regularly see or talk with people I care about and have access to friends, family, neighbors, activities or a community that keeps me engaged. My home doesn't leave me feeling isolated.

**7. Do you have support if something changes?**
If I became sick, stopped driving, needed help around the house or required some assistance with daily life, I know who I could call and what resources are available. My plan doesn't depend entirely on one person.

**8. Does staying here still make financial sense for the life you want?**
I understand what my home truly costs me, including taxes, insurance, utilities, maintenance and upcoming repairs. I'm comfortable with those costs and with the money I may need to spend to make the home work for me in the future.

**9. Would your home still work after an unexpected change?**
If my health, mobility, finances or support system changed, I believe I would have enough options and time to make thoughtful decisions rather than being forced into a crisis move.

### Question 10: The Big Question

Please visually distinguish this final question from the others.

**If you were choosing today, would you choose this home again?**

Knowing what you know today, if you were choosing a home for the next 5 to 10 years of your life, would you choose this one?

Possible answers:

* **Absolutely**
* **I'm not sure**
* **Probably not**

### Results

Please do **not** create a simplistic numerical “stay” or “move” score.

Instead, use the responses to show the visitor where the major areas of their life fall:

**Working Well**
**Worth Planning For**
**Needs a Closer Look**

Then provide an appropriate overall result using one of these three messages:

### Working Well

**Your home and your life seem to be working well together.**

That doesn't mean you shouldn't plan. In fact, planning while things are going well gives you the most choices. Your answers can help you identify a few things worth preparing for now so you can continue living the way you want.

### Worth Planning For

**Your home may still be a good fit, but a few things deserve some attention.**

You may not need a different home. You may need a plan. Home modifications, transportation, maintenance help, community resources or other support could make a meaningful difference.

### Time to Explore

**It may be worth exploring what else is possible.**

That doesn't mean you need to move. It means several parts of your current situation may be requiring more from you than you'd like. Understanding your options now can help you make decisions on your terms rather than waiting until circumstances make them for you.

### Closing for Every Result

**Sometimes staying means changing the house. Sometimes it means changing houses. Either way, it starts with a plan.**

CTA:

Link this CTA to Mary's existing 30-minute conversation/appointment option.

Visitors should **not be required to provide contact information before receiving their results.**

If technically appropriate, an optional **Email Me My Results** function may be offered *after* the results are displayed, but it should not gate the assessment.
