# 4. ONE STEP AT A TIME

### Heading

# One Step at a Time.

### Supporting Line

You don’t have to solve everything today. We put the pieces in the right order.

Create six numbered editorial steps using the visual language established by the “Three Ways to Begin” cards on the Home page.

Use warm cream or warm-white cards, thin navy framing, restrained antique-gold accents, subtle depth, and generous padding.

Do not make them look like tech/SaaS cards.

## 1. Start With the Conversation

We talk about what’s working, what isn’t, your timeline, your concerns, and what you would like life to look like next.

## 2. Explore Where You’re Going

Before deciding what stays or goes, we look at your options.

That might mean a smaller home, condo, villa, 55+ community, senior living community, multigenerational home, or even staying where you are.

We consider location, lifestyle, budget, maintenance, accessibility, proximity to family, healthcare, and the things that matter most to you.

## 3. Build the Plan

Once we understand the destination, we create a realistic timeline.

What needs to happen first?
What can wait?
Who needs to be involved?

Instead of one enormous project, we create manageable steps.

## 4. Decide What Comes With You

Now we look at the new space.

What furniture fits?
What storage is available?
What do you use and love?
What will actually serve you in your next home?

We make decisions based on **where you are going**, not simply on what you currently own.

## 5. Handle What’s Left

Some things will go with family. Some may be donated, sold, recycled, digitized, or simply let go.

Mary can connect clients with trusted local professionals who can help with sorting, estate sales, donations, clean-outs, moving, repairs, and other pieces of the transition.

## 6. Prepare, Sell, and Get Settled

If selling the current home is part of the plan, Mary manages the real estate piece from preparation and pricing through settlement.

The goal is not simply to get the house sold. It is to get the client comfortably to the other side of the move.
